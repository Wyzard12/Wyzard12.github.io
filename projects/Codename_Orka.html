Cinematic Short Film / 3D Animation Prototype

CODENAME ORKA

IN SHORT

Orka marks my directorial debut into 3D cinematic filmmaking, translating a concept originally formulated during my third-year internship into a fully realized animated short for my final graduation exam at SintLucas. Venturing into digital animation with zero prior experience, I established an independent pipeline from the ground up—deconstructing the Unity animation interface, teaching myself keyframe manipulation, and reverse-engineering prefab assets. Inspired by the tactical atmosphere of Rainbow Six Siege, this project showcases my resourcefulness in converting strict asset limitations into creative visual workarounds.

PROJECT OVERVIEW & THE BOTTOM-UP APPROACH

The vision for Orka was born out of a long-standing desire to master cinematic pacing, camera composition, and visual storytelling—skills that directly elevate game design domains like cutscene direction and environmental narrative.

Lacking formal training in animation, I adopted a strict bottom-up learning methodology. I opened Unity and thoroughly experimented with the interface, diving deep into how keyframes operate, how timeline interpolation behaves, and how camera angles dictate psychological tension. For the visual assets, I sourced low-poly models from the Synty ecosystem and paired them with character rigs from Mixamo, building upon foundational rigging concepts I had observed in prior development cycles. This phase of technical exploration served as my foundation before transitioning into actual short-film production.

CINEMATIC TRANSLATION & CREATIVE WORKAROUNDS

The primary production bottleneck surfaced when the Mixamo database lacked the specific, advanced animations required for my script—particularly zero-gravity space flotation. Rather than altering the creative vision or succumbing to technical constraints, I engineered a creative workaround. I sourced a traditional swimming animation and heavily manipulated its interpolation curves, drastically stretching and slowing down the asset keyframes to convincingly simulate the weightless drift of zero gravity.

To capture the specific high-tension atmosphere of the Rainbow Six Siege: Operation Void Edge trailer, camera positioning became paramount. I meticulously matched the focal lengths, framing cuts, and tracking angles of the source material to mask the lack of bespoke character animations. Once individual scenes were baked in Unity, I imported the raw sequences into Adobe Premiere Pro, stitching the shots together to establish precise narrative continuity and tight rhythmic cuts.
